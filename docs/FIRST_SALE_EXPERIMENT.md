# First-sale experiment: One Bottle at a Time

Observed October 4: Gumroad returned zero successful sales. The two recent offer-bearing videos had 1,006 and 810 views at lookup. These are plays, not unique buyers or product visits. Windsor analytics returned older October 1 rows only; October 3 retention is not yet established.

## Offer and audience

For adults learning home cocktails: a $9, 51-page PDF with a six-bottle plan and 15 recipes. Keep the existing price and product. No income, skill, cost-saving or scarcity guarantees. The paid download still requires independent buyer-path/seller-artifact verification.

## Seven-day organic experiment

Use the normal daily publishing schedule, not an increase in volume. Prioritize home-bar shopping and recipe-planning problems, then make the ebook offer specific. Three reviewed editorial lessons cover starting with one bottle, planning six bottles and choosing recipes with shared ingredients. The finite library does not reuse archived lessons.

Future upload links use the workflow run and attempt as an identifier known before upload. The receipt stores the same URL actually posted, instead of a different URL constructed afterward. Existing October 3 descriptions were repaired to use their actual video IDs. The channel-description link has separate attribution. Shorts description links may not be clickable; the spoken CTA directs viewers to the channel description. Verify the actual mobile purchase path.

## Decision evidence

Track date, video ID, publication URL, posted product URL, views, engaged views/retention when available, actual product visits when available, captured orders, refunds and known costs. Missing visit data means purchase conversion is unknown. UTM links alone do not prove attribution is retained by checkout receipts.

Review after seven days. With no product visits, test offer placement and the mobile channel path. With observed visits but no purchases, test the product preview and buyer objections. With purchases, verify delivery and net economics before increasing effort. With refunds or delivery failures, fix those before more acquisition. Do not scale paid acquisition or extrapolate profit from views.
