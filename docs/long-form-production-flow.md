# Long-form pilot production flow

The long-form pilot is developed on `media-engine-v2-story`. The daily Behind The Bar Shorts schedule on `main` continues independently. The pilot has no automatic public publishing trigger.

Eric approved the existing *Midnight Archive* concept and selected a cinematic motion comic for the pilot. Preserve the established characters, mystery, and Sublevel 7 ending while revising continuity and expanding the script.

## Current artifact

`pilots/midnight-archive/s01e01-screenplay.json` is a seven-scene story outline with 50 dialogue lines (approximately 345 spoken words), not a production-ready 13-minute screenplay. The story bible, characters, episode concept, continuity store, and writers-room prompt are present. The pilot has not been voiced, illustrated, edited, rendered, uploaded, or published.

## Sequence and release gates

1. **Creative direction:** Concept and motion-comic format are approved. Keep the established story and resolve the concrete draft issues below.
2. **Script revision:** Expand the outline into a complete scene-by-scene script. Give actions and silences explicit durations; verify the assembled estimated runtime from timed dialogue and shots, not the `runtime_target_minutes` field. Resolve continuity and timing errors before production.
3. **Production plan:** Lock each scene's dialogue, narration, visual prompts, shot list, timing, audio cues, and character voice assignments. Every major physical action needs a sequence of consistent character poses or a layered rig; camera pans alone do not satisfy character-motion review. Track source and usage rights for every asset.
4. **Private pilot:** Generate voices and visuals, assemble and caption a playable video, then verify audio, visuals, subtitles, runtime, narrative coherence, and rights. Upload privately only after local quality review.
5. **Owner review:** Provide Eric the private video and revision notes. Make requested changes, then ask for his approval of the actual finished episode before any public release.

## Specific draft notes

- The watch is introduced at 2:47 AM with **47 minutes** left, but the script says it reaches zero at 3:47 AM, **60 minutes** later. Adjust the countdown, introduction time, or payoff time consistently.
- In scene 5 the elevator descends to Sublevel 7. In scene 7 it rises to Mara on Sublevel 3 and opens directly onto a Sublevel 7 corridor. Clarify whether the characters enter and descend, or whether the elevator doorway itself crosses into the impossible floor.
- The current 345 spoken words would take roughly two to three minutes at a typical dramatic pace, before pauses and visual action. A 13-minute target requires timed action and substantially more developed scenes. Do not stretch the existing dialogue with artificial pauses merely to hit the target.
- The cold open is summarized, but has no timed production shots. The final script needs an explicit hook, visual sequence, and timing for the opening seconds.

## Creative review prompts

The concept and cinematic motion-comic format are settled. Eric reviews the private playable pilot for pacing, character performance, visuals, and sound before public release; approval of the concept is not approval of an unfinished video.
